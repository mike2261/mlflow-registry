# Open-weight English speech-to-text (STT/ASR) models — state as of September 2026

Scope: open-weight, self-hostable English ASR. Vietnamese is covered by another researcher. Dates in this note matter: the field moved fast in H1 2026 (Qwen3-ASR Jan, Nemotron Speech Jan/Mar/Jun, Voxtral Realtime Feb, Granite 4.0/4.1 Speech Mar/Apr, Cohere Transcribe Mar, ARK-ASR-3B May). Anything from 2024 or earlier is marked "older".

## Key Question 1: Which open-weight models lead English benchmarks right now?

### Takeaway
On the Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard (English short-form, 8 test sets, mean WER) the top open-weight tier in mid-2026 is a cluster of ~2–3B "speech encoder + LLM decoder" models separated by well under one WER point: MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B (4.87, per its own card), ARK-ASR-3B (5.04, but computed on 7 sets, excluding TED-LIUM), Granite Speech 4.1 2B (5.33), Cohere Transcribe 2B (5.42), Granite 4.0 1B Speech (5.52), NVIDIA Canary-Qwen-2.5B (5.63), Qwen3-ASR-1.7B (5.76), Granite Speech 3.3 8B (5.85). Whisper large-v3 (7.44) is now ~2 WER points behind the leaders and is an "older" model; the fast CTC/TDT models (Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2/v3 at 6.05/6.34) sit in between with 10–100x the throughput.

### Cited Findings
- Leaderboard methodology: the Open ASR Leaderboard evaluates "60+ open and closed-source models" across 11 datasets, reporting WER for accuracy and inverse real-time factor (RTFx, higher = faster) for efficiency; as of Nov 2025 it added multilingual and long-form tracks — [HF blog, 2025-11-21](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-asr-leaderboard)
- The leaderboard paper (arXiv 2510.06961, final version 2026-03-30) compares "86 open-source and proprietary systems across 12 datasets" and finds "Conformer-based encoders paired with transformer-based decoders achieve the best average WER, while connectionist temporal classification (CTC) and token-and-duration transducer (TDT) decoders offer superior RTFx" — [arXiv 2510.06961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06961)
- The leaderboard repo now runs evaluations on standardized hardware ("1x H200 (141 GB)") via Hugging Face Jobs; it has evaluation folders for Transformers, NeMo, ESPnet, SpeechBrain, CTranslate2, TensorRT-LLM plus vendor-specific ones (Qwen, Granite, Kyutai) — [github.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard](https://github.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard)
- The HF blog states CTC and TDT decoders achieve "10–100× faster throughput" than LLM-based systems; on long-form, Parakeet CTC 1.1B reaches RTFx 2793.75 vs 68.56 for Whisper large-v3 — [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-asr-leaderboard)

### Inferences
- For pure English offline accuracy per GB, the 2026 sweet spot is a ~2B Apache-2.0 model (Granite Speech 4.1 2B, Cohere Transcribe, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B). They beat Whisper large-v3 by roughly 2 absolute WER points on the leaderboard mean while using similar or less VRAM.
- Leaderboard means from different model cards are not strictly comparable (dataset subsets, normalizer versions, evaluation dates). Treat differences under ~0.3 WER as noise; re-benchmark on your own audio.
- LibriSpeech test-clean is saturated (1.0–1.6% for all leaders); AMI and Earnings22 (meetings, far-field, finance jargon) are the discriminating sets and the ones most like real-world call/meeting audio.

### Gaps
- The live Open ASR Leaderboard Space is JS-rendered and could not be scraped; per-model numbers above come from each model card's leaderboard block or third-party snapshots (Jan and Jul 2026). I could not confirm the exact ordering as of late September 2026.
- Meta Omnilingual ASR (omniASR-LLM-7B, 7.8B params) publishes no English WER on its model card; a third-party snippet cites 6.87% on the leaderboard but I could not verify it on a primary source — [HF: facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B)
- Kyutai's "6.4" figure is labelled inconsistently (model card block vs LibriSpeech); it is most likely the leaderboard mean, not LibriSpeech-clean.
- MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B's 4.87 average lists only 6 datasets on its card; whether TED-LIUM and LS-other are included is unclear.

## Key Question 2: Per-model fact sheet (WER, size, license, latency, streaming, hardware, HF repo, file format)

### Takeaway
Licenses split into three buckets: Apache-2.0 (Qwen3-ASR, Granite Speech 4.x, Cohere Transcribe, ARK-ASR-3B, MOSS-Transcribe, Voxtral, Omnilingual ASR, wav2vec2), CC-BY-4.0 attribution-required (all NVIDIA Canary/Parakeet, Kyutai STT), MIT (Whisper per GitHub, Distil-Whisper, Phi-4-multimodal, Moonshine current models), and NVIDIA's newer Nemotron streaming models under NVIDIA Open Model License / OpenMDW-1.1. Only Meta SeamlessM4T v2 and MMS are CC-BY-NC-4.0 (non-commercial) and should be excluded from commercial self-hosting.

### Cited Findings

**Granite Speech 4.1 2B (IBM, 2026-04-29) — top Apache-2.0 accuracy**
- License Apache 2.0; 2B params; languages EN/FR/DE/ES/PT/JA; mean WER 5.33, RTFx 231.29; safetensors BF16; runs in Transformers ≥4.52.1, vLLM, llama.cpp, mlx-audio ≥0.4.1; CTC encoder uses "block-attention with 4-seconds audio blocks"; timestamps and speaker attribution only in the `granite-speech-4.1-2b-plus` variant; keyword biasing; punctuation and capitalization — [HF: ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b)
- Trained on 174,000 hours of public + synthetic audio — [IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/blog/granite-4-1-ai-foundation-models)
- `granite-speech-4.1-2b-nar` (April 2026): non-autoregressive "conditional transcript editing" (440M CTC Conformer + 160M Q-Former + 1B bidirectional Granite LLM editor); LS clean 1.29%, RTFx ~1820 on H100; needs Transformers ≥5.5.3, PyTorch ≥2.9.1, Flash Attention 2; limitation: "prioritizes deletions, potentially dropping words in noisy conditions" — [HF: ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b-nar](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b-nar)

**Granite 4.0 1B Speech (IBM, 2026-03-06)**

- Apache 2.0; ~2.4B params (Qwen3-1.7B-base backbone + Qwen3-Omni-MoE audio encoder + gated-MLP adapter); English only; mean 4.87, RTFx 173.26; Transformers `AutoModelForCausalLM` with custom processor; no timestamps in the example (`enable_time_marker=False`); ~4.84 GB bf16 safetensors single shard — [HF: OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B); sibling MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize (0.9B, multi-speaker) is supported in vLLM — [vLLM supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models.html)

**NVIDIA Canary-Qwen-2.5B (Jul 2025)**

### Inferences
- For an MLflow/MinIO registry, safetensors-based models (Granite, Cohere, Qwen3-ASR, MOSS, ARK, Voxtral, Whisper, Kyutai) are the simplest to store and version; NVIDIA .nemo checkpoints are single tar archives and equally storable but pin you to the NeMo runtime and its specific version (2.2 / 2.4 / 2.5 / 25.11 / 26.06 depending on model).
- CC-BY-4.0 (NVIDIA, Kyutai) is commercially usable but requires attribution in your product/docs; Apache-2.0 models avoid that. NVIDIA's 2026 Nemotron streaming models moved off CC-BY to NVIDIA Open Model License / OpenMDW-1.1, which should be read before adoption.
- CPU feasibility: realistically only Moonshine, whisper.cpp small/base/turbo quantized, and Parakeet 0.6B via ONNX/OpenVINO/GGUF are CPU-viable; the 2–3B LLM-decoder models need a GPU (~5–10 GB bf16, ≥16 GB for Voxtral Realtime).

### Gaps
- Exact download sizes are only documented for some models (Voxtral Realtime 8.87 GB, MOSS ~4.84 GB, Moonshine 34/245 MB). For others, estimate ~2 bytes/param in bf16.
- Official per-model GPU VRAM figures are mostly absent from model cards (Canary-Qwen explicitly gives none).
- No primary source found for Omnilingual ASR English WER, Cohere Transcribe streaming support, or exact terms of NVIDIA's OpenMDW-1.1 license.

## Key Question 3: Which inference stacks are common and what does each need?

### Takeaway
Five stacks cover the field: Hugging Face Transformers (broadest; Granite, Cohere, Qwen3-ASR, MOSS, ARK, Whisper, Kyutai, Phi-4), vLLM (production serving with OpenAI-style API for Whisper, Voxtral, Qwen3-ASR, Granite, Cohere, MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize, Phi-4), NVIDIA NeMo (mandatory for Canary/Parakeet/Nemotron .nemo checkpoints; Python ≥3.12, PyTorch ≥2.7), faster-whisper/CTranslate2 (Whisper family only; CUDA 12 + cuDNN 9, int8 option), and whisper.cpp/GGML (Whisper family, CPU/Metal/CUDA/Vulkan, no Python needed).

### Cited Findings
- vLLM lists transcription-capable models: Whisper, Voxtral ("Supports streaming via `/v1/realtime` WebSocket"), Qwen3-ASR, Granite Speech and Granite Speech Plus, MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize, FunASR, FireRedASR2, GLM-ASR, Cohere-Transcribe; plus audio-capable multimodal Phi-4-multimodal, Gemma3n/Gemma4, Qwen3-Omni, Kimi-Audio; "Voxtral Realtime requires `mistral-common[audio]` to be installed, and must be served with `--tokenizer-mode mistral`" — [vLLM supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models.html)
- NeMo Speech (now split into the NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech repo, release 3.0.0): `pip install 'nemo-toolkit[asr,tts]'`, or `uv sync --extra all --extra cu13` from source, or NGC container `nvcr.io/nvidia/nemo-speech:26.07.00`; minimum Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.7; tested stack Python 3.13 / PyTorch 2.12 / CUDA 13.2; some checkpoints need `weights_only=False` (`TORCH_FORCE_NO_WEIGHTS_ONLY_LOAD=1`) on PyTorch ≥2.6 — [GitHub NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech)
- Per-model NeMo pins: Parakeet v2 → NeMo 2.2; v3 → 2.4; Canary-1B-v2 → main until 2.5; Canary-Qwen → ≥2.5.0 + PyTorch 2.6; Nemotron streaming EN → ≥25.11; Nemotron-3.5 → 26.06 — model cards linked in Q2
- faster-whisper (CTranslate2): GPU needs "cuBLAS for CUDA 12" and "cuDNN 9 for CUDA 12" (downgrade CTranslate2 to 3.24.0 for CUDA 11 or 4.4.0 for cuDNN 8); compute types float16, int8_float16, int8 (CPU or GPU); 13-min audio: faster-whisper FP16 1m03s / 4525 MB vs openai-whisper FP16 2m23s / 4708 MB; batched int8 16 s; `BatchedInferencePipeline`; Silero VAD filter removes silence >2 s by default; supports Distil-Whisper and turbo via CTranslate2 conversion — [GitHub SYSTRAN/faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)
- faster-whisper runs "on any NVIDIA GPU from a GTX 1070 upward (6 GB VRAM minimum with INT8 precision)" — [runaihome 2026 guide](https://runaihome.com/blog/whisper-large-v3-self-hosted-transcription-server-2026/)
- whisper.cpp: MIT; custom `ggml` single-file format (weights + mel filters + vocab); integer quantization (e.g. Q5_0); backends CPU/NEON/Accelerate, Metal, Core ML, CUDA, ROCm/HIP, OpenVINO, Vulkan, Ascend CANN, MUSA; memory: tiny 75 MB disk / ~273 MB RAM, base 142 MB / ~388 MB, large 2.9 GB / ~3.9 GB; Silero-VAD integration; streaming mic example via SDL2 — [GitHub ggml-org/whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp)
- Transformers version floors: Granite 4.x ≥4.52.1 (NAR ≥5.5.3), Voxtral Realtime ≥5.2.0, Kyutai ≥4.53, Phi-4 ≥4.48.2, Voxtral-Mini-3B ≥4.54; ARK-ASR needs `trust_remote_code=True` — model cards linked in Q2
- Kyutai ships three implementations: "PyTorch: for research and tinkering", "Rust: for production" (WebSocket streaming), "MLX: for on-device inference on iPhone and Mac" — [kyutai.org/stt](https://kyutai.org/stt/)
- Qwen3-ASR: Transformers for offline, vLLM for speed and streaming; forced aligner separate model — [GitHub QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)
- Omnilingual ASR uses fairseq2 and needs libsndfile — [HF: facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B)
- Cross-model C++/ggml runtimes are emerging: CrispASR covers Cohere Transcribe, Parakeet TDT, Voxtral, Canary 1B v2 — [GitHub CrispStrobe/CrispASR](https://github.com/CrispStrobe/CrispASR); sherpa-onnx has ONNX exports for Parakeet/Omnilingual — [sherpa-onnx issue #3267](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3267)
- Leaderboard eval folders exist for Transformers, NeMo, ESPnet, SpeechBrain, CTranslate2, TensorRT-LLM — [github.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard](https://github.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard)

### Inferences
- A single Python service with Transformers + torch can load every non-NVIDIA leader (Granite, Cohere, Qwen3-ASR, MOSS, ARK, Whisper, Kyutai-trfs), which is the lowest-friction path for an MLflow pyfunc flavor. Adding NeMo is a heavy second dependency tree (Python ≥3.12, its own PyTorch/CUDA pins) that is worth it mainly for Parakeet's ~3300 RTFx or Nemotron's cache-aware streaming.
- vLLM is the natural choice if you want an OpenAI-compatible `/v1/audio/transcriptions`-style API and batched GPU throughput for the LLM-decoder models; Transformers alone will be several times slower for those.
- For CPU-only or edge, whisper.cpp (turbo/distil GGML) or Moonshine are the only mature options; expect WER in the 6.6–7.8 range rather than ~5.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from the vLLM docs excerpt whether `/v1/audio/transcriptions` is supported for every listed model or only Whisper/Voxtral/Qwen3-ASR.
- No sourced benchmark of Transformers vs vLLM throughput for the 2B Granite/Cohere models.

## Key Question 4: Known self-hosting gotchas

### Takeaway
Whisper-family models hallucinate on silence/non-speech and loop on repeated text unless a VAD front-end (Silero via faster-whisper/whisper.cpp) and `condition_on_previous_text=False` are used; LLM-decoder models are capped at short inputs (30–40 s for ARK/Canary-Qwen/Phi-4) and need app-side chunking; Parakeet's long-form "local attention" mode and non-greedy decoding have documented failure modes; several top models (Cohere, ARK, MOSS) emit no timestamps at all, so timestamps must come from a separate aligner (Qwen3-ForcedAligner) or a TDT/Whisper model.

### Cited Findings
- Whisper large-v3 card: "the predictions may include texts that are not actually spoken in the audio input (i.e. hallucination)" and the seq2seq architecture "makes it prone to generating repetitive texts"; long-form handled by sequential (slower, more accurate) or chunked (`chunk_length_s=30`) algorithms — [HF: openai/whisper-large-v3](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3); turbo card repeats the same warnings and notes "disparate performance on different accents and dialects" — [HF: openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo)
- Calm-Whisper (2025): only 3 of 20 decoder self-attention heads in large-v3 "account for over 75% of hallucinations" on non-speech; fine-tuning them gives ">80% reduction in non-speech hallucination with only less than 0.1% WER degradation on LibriSpeech" — [arXiv 2505.12969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12969)
- Whisper was trained on 30-second clips; silence or noise makes it emit subtitle-like text from training priors; practical fixes: `vad_filter=True` (Silero VAD) in faster-whisper, `beam_size=1`, `condition_on_previous_text=False`, and trimming 0.5 s at chunk boundaries when stitching — [DEV: Whisper hallucination on silence](https://dev.to/nareshipme/whisper-hallucination-on-silence-why-your-transcript-loops-the-same-phrase-2pg4); [openai/whisper discussion #1606](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/1606); [openai/whisper discussion #679](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/679)
- faster-whisper's VAD filter removes silence longer than 2 s by default (`min_silence_duration_ms` tunable) — [GitHub SYSTRAN/faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper); whisper.cpp also integrates Silero-VAD — [GitHub ggml-org/whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp)
- Distil-Whisper claims lower long-form hallucination than the original Whisper — [LiteASR / distil references via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.20583)
- Input-length ceilings: ARK-ASR-3B max 30 s (480,000 samples) — [HF: Audio8/ARK-ASR-3B](https://huggingface.co/Audio8/ARK-ASR-3B); Canary-Qwen trained to 40 s — [HF: nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b); Phi-4-multimodal "Maximum audio length is suggested to be 40s" — [HF: microsoft/Phi-4-multimodal-instruct](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-multimodal-instruct); Cohere Transcribe auto-chunks above `max_audio_clip_s` — [HF: CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026](https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026); Voxtral-Mini-3B-2507 transcribes up to 30 min — [HF](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-3B-2507)
- Parakeet long-form: v3 supports 24 min full attention (A100 80 GB) or 3 h with local attention — [HF: nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3); users with 12 GB VRAM ask how to do >1 h audio — [HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2/discussions/15); one practitioner reports single-pass long audio "silently drops content" and recommends an app-side 12-min window + 2-min overlap chunker with word-LCS stitching on 8 GB consumer GPUs — [dosmoon aistack](https://dosmoon.com/aistack/research/parakeet-on-consumer-gpu/) (single blog; treat as anecdotal)
- Parakeet decoding: in sherpa-onnx, `modified_beam_search` with parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3 returned hallucinated text like "Yeah." or empty output ~20% of the time while `greedy_search` was correct — [sherpa-onnx issue #3267](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3267); LocalAI reported empty transcriptions with nemo-parakeet-tdt-0.6b — [LocalAI issue #8682](https://github.com/mudler/LocalAI/issues/8682)
- Parakeet noise sensitivity: WER 6.05 → 6.95 at SNR 10 and 20.26 at SNR −5 — [HF: nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2)
- Timestamps: not supported by Cohere Transcribe, ARK-ASR-3B, MOSS-Transcribe-preview (example uses `enable_time_marker=False`); Granite 4.1 timestamps only in the `-plus` variant; Qwen3-ASR streaming mode has no timestamps and needs the separate ForcedAligner (11 languages, ≤5 min); Parakeet TDT gives char/word/segment timestamps natively; Whisper gives sentence/word timestamps — model cards linked above
- Punctuation/casing: Parakeet, Nemotron, Kyutai, Granite, Whisper produce punctuated, cased text; wav2vec2 and MMS produce lowercase unpunctuated text — [HF: facebook/wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self](https://huggingface.co/facebook/wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self); [HF: facebook/mms-1b-all](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all)
- Granite 4.1 2B-NAR "prioritizes deletions, potentially dropping words in noisy conditions" — [HF: ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b-nar](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b-nar)
- Canary-Qwen "cannot preserve underlying LLM capabilities when processing speech" and multilingual output is unreliable despite the multilingual encoder — [HF: nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b)
- Nemotron streaming accuracy degrades as latency drops (6.93% at 1.12 s → 8.43% at 80 ms), so latency is a tunable accuracy knob — [HF: nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b); Voxtral Realtime likewise recommends 480 ms delay as the balance point — [HF: mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602)
- Kyutai's semantic VAD (end-of-turn detection) exists only in the Rust server — [kyutai.org/stt](https://kyutai.org/stt/)
- NeMo checkpoints may require `weights_only=False` on PyTorch ≥2.6 — [GitHub NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech)
- Whisper HF card license metadata (Apache 2.0) disagrees with the MIT license in the openai/whisper repo — [HF: openai/whisper-large-v3](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3); [GitHub openai/whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper) — record the license you rely on in the MLflow model metadata.

### Inferences
- A robust pipeline for arbitrary-length English audio is: VAD segmentation (Silero) → 20–30 s chunks with overlap → batched inference on a ≤30 s-capped LLM-decoder model (or Parakeet TDT for speed) → stitch. Doing this in your own service avoids relying on each model's internal long-form mode, which is the least-tested code path.
- If word timestamps are a hard requirement, pick Parakeet TDT (native), Whisper/faster-whisper (native), Granite 4.1 2B-plus, or pair any LLM-decoder model with Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B.
- Benchmark WER on your domain audio: the leaderboard's hardest sets (AMI 8–17%, Earnings22 8–12%) show real-world meeting/call WER is 5–10x LibriSpeech-clean WER for every model.

### Gaps
- No systematic, sourced hallucination-rate comparison across the 2026 LLM-decoder models (Granite/Cohere/Qwen3-ASR/MOSS/ARK) on silence or music; only Whisper has been studied in depth.
- No primary documentation of maximum input length for MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B or Qwen3-ASR offline mode.
