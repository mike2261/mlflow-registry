# Permissive speech models now rival closed APIs, except in Vietnamese TTS

As of September 2026 the strongest self-hostable English speech-to-text model is a ~2B-parameter Apache-2.0 "speech encoder + LLM decoder" model: **IBM Granite Speech 4.1 2B scores 5.33% mean WER on the Open ASR Leaderboard**, with Cohere Transcribe (5.42), Qwen3-ASR-1.7B (5.76) and MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B (4.87) all within a point, and all of them beat **Whisper large-v3 (7.44%) by roughly two absolute WER points** at similar VRAM. Vietnamese STT has two commercially usable front-runners, Alibaba's **Qwen3-ASR-1.7B** (Apache-2.0, FLEURS-vi WER 5.55) and NVIDIA's **parakeet-ctc-0.6b-Vietnamese** (NVIDIA Open Model License, FLEURS 5.15, with punctuation, timestamps and streaming), while the most accurate small model, ChunkFormer-large-vie, is CC-BY-NC and blocked for commercial use. English TTS is the quadrant where quality and license diverge hardest: the three highest-ranked open-weight models on the Artificial Analysis arena (Breeze TTS 2, Fish S2 Pro, Voxtral TTS) are all non-commercial, leaving **Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0), Chatterbox (MIT) and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B (Apache-2.0)** as the production shortlist. Vietnamese TTS is effectively a one-model field for commercial deployment: none of the big multilingual open models list Vietnamese, and among Vietnamese-specific releases only **VieNeu-TTS v3 Turbo (Apache-2.0, 0.1B params, 48 kHz, cloning and streaming)** escapes the CC-BY-NC licenses inherited from the viVoice and PhoAudiobook training corpora. For a registry that pulls weights from Hugging Face into MinIO and serves them from Python, safetensors checkpoints loaded by Transformers or vLLM (Granite, Qwen3-ASR, Qwen3-TTS, VieNeu) are the lowest-friction artifacts, whereas NVIDIA `.nemo` archives and F5/XTTS forks pin you to heavier, version-locked runtimes. The single most useful simplification: **Qwen3-ASR-1.7B can serve both English and Vietnamese from one registered model**, and every TTS pick needs an external text normalizer and sentence chunker in front of it.

## English STT: Apache-2.0 2B models beat Whisper by two WER points

The English field reorganized in the first half of 2026. The Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard, which now evaluates "60+ open and closed-source models" across 11 datasets on standardized H200 hardware and reports both WER and inverse real-time factor (RTFx), shows a top tier of 2–3B models separated by well under one WER point ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-asr-leaderboard); [leaderboard repo](https://github.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard)). The leaderboard paper's finding is the key architectural fact: "Conformer-based encoders paired with transformer-based decoders achieve the best average WER, while CTC and TDT decoders offer superior RTFx" ([arXiv 2510.06961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06961)). In practice that means you choose between an LLM-decoder model for accuracy and a CTC/TDT model for 10–100x the throughput.

**Granite Speech 4.1 2B** (released 2026-04-29) is the best-documented Apache-2.0 leader: mean WER 5.33, RTFx 231, six languages, safetensors BF16, and it runs in Transformers ≥4.52.1, vLLM, llama.cpp and mlx-audio with keyword biasing and punctuation built in ([HF: ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b)). IBM trained it on 174,000 hours of public and synthetic audio and describes it as "among the top models on the OpenASR Leaderboard" ([IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/blog/granite-4-1-ai-foundation-models)). Timestamps only come with the `granite-speech-4.1-2b-plus` variant. **Cohere Transcribe** (2026-03-26, Apache-2.0, 2B) is faster at RTFx 525 with mean WER 5.42 across all eight sets and covers 14 languages including Vietnamese, but "does not feature" timestamps or diarization and its streaming support is undocumented ([HF: CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026](https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026)). Two models post lower averages but with caveats: **MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B** reports 4.87 and RTFx 173 on its own card, listing only six datasets ([HF: OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-Transcribe-preview-2B)), and **ARK-ASR-3B** reports 5.04 over seven sets that exclude TED-LIUM, with a hard 30-second input cap and `trust_remote_code=True` ([HF: Audio8/ARK-ASR-3B](https://huggingface.co/Audio8/ARK-ASR-3B)). As MarkTechPost put it, "the models currently listed side by side were not all scored the same way" ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/23/best-open-speech-recognition-asr-models-in-2026-wer-languages-latency-and-license-compared/)), so differences under ~0.3 WER are noise.

**Qwen3-ASR-1.7B** (Apache-2.0, 2026-01-29) sits at 5.76 mean WER with 52 languages and language ID, runs in Transformers or vLLM (streaming only via vLLM), and pairs with a separate Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B for timestamps ([GitHub QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR); [HF: Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B)). Its distinctive value in this project is that the same checkpoint is a Vietnamese front-runner (next section). NVIDIA's **Canary-Qwen-2.5B** (CC-BY-4.0) scores 5.63 but is English-only, `.nemo`-format, and trained on ≤40 s segments ([HF: nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b)).

The throughput tier is NVIDIA's **Parakeet TDT 0.6B**: v2 (English-only, CC-BY-4.0) at 6.05 mean WER and RTFx 3,386, with char/word/segment timestamps and documented 8 kHz telephony performance of 6.32% ([HF: nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2)); v3 adds 25 European languages at 6.34 / RTFx 3,333 ([HF: nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)). On long-form audio Parakeet CTC 1.1B reaches RTFx 2,794 versus 68.6 for Whisper large-v3 ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-asr-leaderboard)). For true low-latency streaming, three options stand out: **Nemotron Speech Streaming EN 0.6B** (NVIDIA Open Model License, cache-aware FastConformer + RNNT) trades accuracy for latency along a documented curve from 6.93% WER at 1.12 s chunks to 8.43% at 80 ms ([HF: nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b)); **Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime** (Apache-2.0, 8.87 GB download, ≥16 GB GPU) offers "infinite" streaming with a configurable 80 ms–2.4 s delay, 480 ms recommended, at 7.68 mean WER ([HF: mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602)); and **Kyutai stt-2.6b-en** (CC-BY-4.0) ships a production Rust WebSocket server with a 2.5 s delay ([HF: kyutai/stt-2.6b-en](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-2.6b-en)).

Whisper is now the "older" baseline. large-v3 (Nov 2023) scores 7.44 mean WER at RTFx 146 and large-v3-turbo 7.83 at RTFx 200 ([HF: openai/whisper-large-v3](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3); [HF: openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo)); no "Whisper v4" open release exists ([openai/whisper releases](https://github.com/openai/whisper/releases)). Whisper's remaining advantages are ecosystem depth (faster-whisper, whisper.cpp with Q5 quantization and Silero VAD) and native word timestamps ([GitHub SYSTRAN/faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper); [GitHub ggml-org/whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp)), but its model card itself warns that "predictions may include texts that are not actually spoken" and that it is "prone to generating repetitive texts" ([HF: openai/whisper-large-v3](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3)). Note also that its HF card says Apache-2.0 while the GitHub repo says MIT ([GitHub openai/whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper)); record whichever you rely on in the registry. For CPU-only inference, only Moonshine (MIT for current models; Medium Streaming at 245 MB and 6.65% WER) and quantized whisper.cpp are mature ([GitHub moonshine-ai/moonshine](https://github.com/moonshine-ai/moonshine); [modelslab](https://modelslab.com/blog/audio-generation/moonshine-vs-whisper-asr-real-time-speech-2026)).

Licensing in English STT is a non-problem: only Meta's SeamlessM4T v2 and MMS-1b-all are CC-BY-NC ([HF: facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large); [HF: facebook/mms-1b-all](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all)), and neither is competitive (MMS scores 22.54 English WER). The NVIDIA models require CC-BY-4.0 attribution, and the 2026 Nemotron models moved to the NVIDIA Open Model License / OpenMDW-1.1, which permits commercial use but terminates on patent litigation or guardrail bypass ([NVIDIA Open Model License](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/agreements/enterprise-software/nvidia-open-model-license/)).

## Vietnamese STT: two permissive leaders, but the best model per parameter is non-commercial

No public leaderboard ranks Vietnamese ASR models on identical normalization. The Open ASR Leaderboard's multilingual track covers only DE/FR/IT/ES/PT ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-asr-leaderboard)), and the community Vietnamese Open ASR Leaderboard's results file contained only empty headers when downloaded on 26 Sep 2026 ([VietAudio-team dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/VietAudio-team/Vietnamese-asr-leaderboard)). The evidence is therefore a patchwork of model cards and 2025–2026 papers, and cross-source WER numbers are only indicative. What is unambiguous is that vanilla Whisper large-v3 is far behind on Vietnamese: FLEURS-vi 8.59, Common Voice 13.74 and GigaSpeech 2 test 17.94 as measured by the GigaSpeech 2 authors ([GigaSpeech 2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11546)), and VIVOS 8.81 / Common Voice 15.45 / VLSP2020-T1 20.41 on the ChunkFormer card ([HF: khanhld/chunkformer-large-vie](https://huggingface.co/khanhld/chunkformer-large-vie)).

Two commercially usable models lead. **Qwen3-ASR-1.7B** lists Vietnamese explicitly among its 30 languages and reports **FLEURS-vi WER 5.55** (8.52 for the 0.6B variant) and MLC-SLM Vietnamese 14.92 in its technical report ([Qwen3-ASR Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1); [HF: Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B)). **NVIDIA parakeet-ctc-0.6b-Vietnamese** is a 600M FastConformer-CTC trained on ~2,000 hours across 10 public Vietnamese datasets and is the only 2026 model that publishes a wide spread of Vietnamese numbers: **FLEURS 5.15, VIVOS 5.96, Common Voice 20 9.73, VLSP 2021 T1 6.86 and T2 8.99, GigaSpeech 2 11.23, ViMD (multi-dialect) 11.02, VietMed 13.6–14.5**, with char/word/segment timestamps and optional KenLM decoding ([HF: nvidia/parakeet-ctc-0.6b-Vietnamese](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-ctc-0.6b-Vietnamese)). Its NIM packaging describes it as a "Vietnamese English code switch model" supporting streaming and offline transcription that "can transcribe some popular English words, but not all English audio" ([NVIDIA NGC catalog](https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nim/teams/nvidia/containers/parakeet-ctc-0.6b-vi)). The trade-off is runtime: Parakeet ships as a `.nemo` archive that requires the NeMo toolkit at serve time, while Qwen3-ASR is a standard safetensors checkpoint with native Transformers support since June 2026 and vLLM for streaming ([QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/blob/main/README.md)).

The model that wins on accuracy per parameter cannot be deployed commercially. **ChunkFormer-large-vie** (Zalo-affiliated, ICASSP 2025) is a 110M CTC model under **CC-BY-NC-4.0** that posts VIVOS 4.18 / Common Voice 6.66 / VLSP T1 14.09 (average 8.31) against PhoWhisper-large's 4.67 / 8.14 / 13.75 (8.85) under the same normalization, with an `endless_decode` mode for long audio on low-memory GPUs ([HF: khanhld/chunkformer-large-vie](https://huggingface.co/khanhld/chunkformer-large-vie)). The Qualcomm PhoASR paper's same-test-set comparison confirms it: ChunkFormer's normalized WER of 6.89 beats PhoWhisper-small (8.97) and PhoASR-whisper-small-3100h (8.20), but its orthographic WER is 32.43% because it emits no punctuation or casing ([PhoASR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14779)). That O-WER/N-WER gap is the defining gotcha of this quadrant: classic Vietnamese benchmarks are lowercase and unpunctuated, so older models (PhoWhisper, wav2vec2, ChunkFormer) look excellent on normalized WER but produce text that needs a downstream punctuation model, whereas the 2026 generation (Parakeet-vi, PhoASR, Qwen3-ASR) outputs punctuated, cased text.

The permissive alternatives are older or narrower. VinAI's **PhoWhisper-large** (BSD-3-Clause, 1.55B, 2024) scores VIVOS 4.67 / Common Voice 8.14 / VLSP2020-T2 26.68 ([PhoWhisper GitHub](https://github.com/VinAIResearch/PhoWhisper/blob/main/README.md); [HF: vinai/PhoWhisper-large](https://huggingface.co/vinai/PhoWhisper-large)) and has a community CTranslate2 conversion for faster-whisper ([HF: quocphu/PhoWhisper-ct2-FasterWhisper](https://huggingface.co/quocphu/PhoWhisper-ct2-FasterWhisper)). **PhoASR-whisper-small** (Qualcomm, EACL 2026, 0.2B) produces "clean transcripts with reliable timestamps, punctuation and capitalization" but carries a dual "BSD 3-Clause Clear and Qualcomm Responsible AI License" and warns of "reduced accuracy for Central Vietnamese speakers" ([HF: Qualcomm-AI-Research/PhoASR-whisper-small](https://huggingface.co/Qualcomm-AI-Research/PhoASR-whisper-small)). **BuzzASR/vietnamese** (MIT, Sept 2026) is a Whisper-large-v3 fine-tune at FLEURS-vi WER 7.26, but it was trained on FLEURS so that number is in-domain ([HF: BuzzASR/vietnamese](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/vietnamese)). Meta's Omnilingual ASR supports Vietnamese under Apache-2.0 but publishes no Vietnamese score, and its 7B LLM variant is a 30 GiB download with RTF 0.092 on an A100 ([HF: facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B)), making it a poor default for a microservice. Cohere Transcribe lists Vietnamese among its 14 languages but publishes no Vietnamese WER ([HF: CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026](https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026)).

Dialect and code-switching are the differentiators that the headline numbers hide. On the ViMD multi-dialect corpus, "the Central region consistently demonstrated the highest error rates": PhoWhisper-base zero-shot scores 14.96 North vs 24.15 Central vs 17.87 South ([ViMD paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03458)). Parakeet-vi is the only 2026 model with a published ViMD number (11.02). For embedded English terms, the ViMedCSS benchmark found the opposite ranking from general accuracy: Whisper-large-v3 had the best code-switch WER (46.69) while PhoWhisper-large and VietASR had the best overall WER, because "broad multilingual exposure improves recognition of embedded English 'islands'" ([ViMedCSS](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12911)). If your audience is Southern or Central, or mixes English heavily, evaluate on ViMD's regional splits and a code-switched sample before committing.

A final due-diligence point: a permissive model license does not guarantee cleared training data. viVoice is CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 ([viVoice GitHub](https://github.com/dieptran43/viVoice)), GigaSpeech 2 Vietnamese is "non-commercial research and educational purposes only" ([GigaSpeech 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11546)), and PhoASR's sources mix "Apache 2.0, CC-BY variants, and custom research licenses" ([PhoASR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14779)). Prefer models whose publishers assert commercial rights outright (NVIDIA, Alibaba, VinAI) and keep the license text inside the MLflow artifact.

## English TTS: the arena leaders are open-weight but not open for business

On the Artificial Analysis Speech Arena, the most-cited blind-listening board of 2026, the open-weight ranking in August 2026 ran **Breeze TTS 2 (1205 Elo), Fish Audio S2 Pro (1122), Step Audio EditX (1095), Voxtral TTS (1078), then NVIDIA Magpie-Multilingual 357M and Kokoro-82M tied at 1063**, followed by Maya1, OpenAudio S1 Mini, Higgs Audio V3 and Chatterbox (1023) ([Artificial Analysis open-weights leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/open-weights)). Breeze TTS 2, released 25 August 2026, became the first open-weight model to outrank ElevenLabs Eleven v3 ([Pinggy blog](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/)), and the gap between best open-weight and best commercial model narrowed from 223 Elo in 2023 to 81 in early 2026 ([OfflineTTS](https://offlinetts.com/blog/tts-arena-leaderboard-2026/)). Elo values shift with votes, so treat them as ±15.

The catch is that the podium is non-commercial. Breeze TTS 2's code is Apache-2.0 but its weights fall under a "BreezeBlue Research and Non-Commercial License": self-hosted outputs cannot be used commercially without written authorization, and a paid BreezeBlue subscription does not grant commercial rights to the weights ([HF: BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2](https://huggingface.co/BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2); [MindStudio license explainer](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/breeze-tts-2-license-commercial-use)). Fish S2 Pro (5B dual-AR, RTF 0.195 and ~100 ms TTFA on an H200) uses the "Fish Audio Research License," and Fish itself says S2 is "open weights, not open source by the OSI definition" ([HF: fishaudio/s2-pro](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro); [Fish Audio blog](https://fish.audio/blog/what-we-mean-by-open-source-for-s2/)). Voxtral TTS (Mistral, ~4B, 70 ms single-request latency, ≥16 GB GPU) is **CC BY-NC 4.0**, with the commercial path being Mistral's API at $0.016 per 1k characters ([HF: mistralai/Voxtral-4B-TTS-2603](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-4B-TTS-2603)). Higgs Audio V3 (Boson, 4B, 102 languages) has its own Research and Non-Commercial License ([HF: bosonai/higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b](https://huggingface.co/bosonai/higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b)). F5-TTS, Spark-TTS, Llasa and XTTS-v2 are likewise non-commercial, and F5's maintainers state the base "cannot be used commercially even after finetuning" ([F5-TTS discussion #997](https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/discussions/997)). The pattern to internalize is "Apache-2.0 badge on GitHub, restrictive license on weights"; always read the license from the weight repo, not the code repo.

The commercially safe tier is small but strong. **Kokoro-82M** (hexgrad, Apache-2.0, Jan 2025) is a StyleTTS 2-architecture model with 54 voices in 8 languages, no voice cloning, ~0.03 RTF on an A100, ~2.4x real-time on CPU and ONNX builds that fit in under 2 GB VRAM or ~4 GB RAM ([HF: hexgrad/Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M); [Pinggy blog](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/)). Its 1063 Elo ties NVIDIA's 357M Magpie, and blind listeners "still pick it over Microsoft's 9-billion-parameter model" ([Pinggy blog](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/)). Kokoro-FastAPI wraps it in an OpenAI-compatible Docker service reporting 35–100x real-time on GPU and ~300 ms first-token latency ([remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI](https://github.com/remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI)). Its limits are a 512-token (~30 s) window that requires chunking under ~500 characters ([deAPI Kokoro guide](https://deapi.ai/blog/kokoro-tts-guide-how-to-control-41-voices-with-nothing-but-punctuation)) and a frozen upstream repo maintained by external forks.

**Chatterbox** (Resemble AI, MIT) is the permissive answer for voice cloning: the 0.5B original clones from 5–10 s of reference, Chatterbox-Turbo (350M) claims sub-200 ms latency with a single-step decoder, Chatterbox-Nano (110M) runs "3x faster than realtime on 8 CPU cores," and Chatterbox-Multilingual V3 covers 23 languages ([resemble-ai/chatterbox GitHub](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox); [HF: ResembleAI/chatterbox](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox)). In a Podonos evaluation listeners preferred it over ElevenLabs in 63.75% of cases ([ActuIA](https://www.actuia.com/en/news/chatterbox-an-open-source-breakthrough-in-speech-synthesis/)). The important constraint is that every output carries Resemble's PerTh neural watermark, which survives MP3 compression with ~100% detection ([Resemble Chatterbox Multilingual v3](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/chatterbox-multilingual-v3-tts-with-embedded-watermarking-for-25-languages)).

**Qwen3-TTS-1.7B** (Apache-2.0, Jan 2026) has no arena score but the best published objective numbers among permissive models: SEED-TTS English WER 1.24 and speaker similarity 0.829, 3-second voice cloning, natural-language voice design, streaming with first-packet latency "as low as 97 ms," and native Transformers support with vLLM-Omni for batch ([QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS); [Qwen blog](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3tts-0115)). **Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B** (Apache-2.0) reports English WER 1.68% and ~150 ms streaming latency with vLLM and TensorRT-LLM runtimes, though its card's disclaimer says "for academic purposes" ([HF: FunAudioLLM/Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512)). **Step Audio EditX** (StepFun, 3B) is the highest-Elo model claiming Apache-2.0, but a Hugging Face discussion found checkpoint text mentioning "academic research purposes" and StepFun's reply only said the license "is governed by the license itself" ([license discussion](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-EditX/discussions/1)); combined with a 30 s per-inference cap and an upstream dormant since April 2026, it is a riskier pick than its ranking suggests. **MOSS-TTS v1.5** (OpenMOSS, Apache-2.0, 8B) is notable for listing both English and Vietnamese among 31 languages, but at 8B parameters and `transformers>=5.0` it is heavy ([HF: OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-TTS-v1.5](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-TTS-v1.5)).

The shared failure mode of every autoregressive LLM-TTS model is long-text collapse. With Qwen3-TTS-0.6B, "the word error rate on prompts with fewer than 500 words is 5.4%, but this increases to 35.2% on concatenated prompts longer than 1500 words" ([arXiv 2609.16989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16989)). Hume's TADA (Feb/Mar 2026) positions its 1:1 text-acoustic alignment as the fix, reporting "zero hallucinations" across 1000+ LibriTTS-R samples, but its 1B model is under the Llama 3.2 Community License ([Hume blog](https://www.hume.ai/blog/opensource-tada); [HF: HumeAI/tada-1b](https://huggingface.co/HumeAI/tada-1b)). A production service must sentence-split and chunk regardless of model.

## Vietnamese TTS: one commercially safe modern model, and it is 0.1B

Vietnamese TTS is the quadrant where the big multilingual models fail outright. XTTS-v2 (17 languages), Chatterbox Multilingual V3 (23 languages), Fish Speech S1 (13), CosyVoice 3 (9 plus Chinese dialects), Qwen3-TTS (10), Kokoro (8) and IndexTTS 2.5 all omit Vietnamese from their official language lists ([HF: coqui/XTTS-v2](https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2); [HF: ResembleAI/chatterbox](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox); [GitHub fishaudio/fish-speech](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech); [HF: FunAudioLLM/Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512); [GitHub QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS)). Community requests for Vietnamese in CosyVoice and Qwen3-TTS have gone unanswered ([CosyVoice issue #1502](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/issues/1502); [Qwen3-TTS discussion #274](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/discussions/274)), and the one 2026 Alibaba model that added Vietnamese, Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS, is API-only ([Alibaba Cloud blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/qwen-audio-3-0-tts-more-multilingual-easier-to-direct_603379)). The exception is MOSS-TTS v1.5, which lists Vietnamese among 31 languages under Apache-2.0 but at 8B parameters is an order of magnitude heavier than anything Vietnamese-specific ([HF: OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-TTS-v1.5](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-TTS-v1.5)).

**VieNeu-TTS v3 Turbo** is the only Vietnamese model that combines a permissive license, a modern architecture and published latency figures. Its card lists **Apache-2.0, 0.1B parameters, BF16 safetensors plus ONNX and PyTorch exports, a from-scratch flow-matching design on the Apache-2.0 MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer-Nano codec, ~10,000 hours of English–Vietnamese training speech, 48 kHz output, 23 preset voices covering North/Central/South regions, voice cloning from 3–8 s clips and 634,482 downloads in the last month** ([HF: pnnbao-ump/VieNeu-TTS-v3-Turbo](https://huggingface.co/pnnbao-ump/VieNeu-TTS-v3-Turbo)). The GitHub repo documents an RTX 3060 TTFA of ~115 ms with RTF 0.10 single-stream (0.011–0.02 batched) and up to 16 concurrent streams, a torch-free ONNX CPU path at RTF 0.35–0.37 int8, an OpenAI-compatible streaming endpoint, LoRA fine-tuning, Vi–En code-switching and sea-g2p text normalization ([GitHub pnnbao97/VieNeu-TTS](https://github.com/pnnbao97/VieNeu-TTS)). Its weaknesses are that the training data is gated and undisclosed, no MOS or WER is published, the int8 CPU path "requires VNNI" or "older CPUs may produce garbled audio," and the author's best model, v4, is proprietary and available only through the vieneu.io API, so the open line may lag the commercial one ([GitHub pnnbao97/VieNeu-TTS](https://github.com/pnnbao97/VieNeu-TTS)).

Everything else in the Vietnamese field is non-commercial because of its training data. The viVoice corpus (1,017 hours scraped from 186 YouTube channels) is **CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0, research only** ([HF: datasets/capleaf/viVoice](https://huggingface.co/datasets/capleaf/viVoice)), and PhoAudiobook (941 hours from 2,697 audiobooks) is released for non-commercial use ([arXiv 2506.01322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01322)). Those two datasets underpin **Viterbox** (Chatterbox fine-tuned on 3,000+ hours, 520M T3 backbone, CC BY-NC 4.0, ~6 GB VRAM) ([GitHub iamdinhthuan/viterbox-tts](https://github.com/iamdinhthuan/viterbox-tts)), the **hynt F5-TTS-Vietnamese-ViVoice** checkpoint (~1,000 hours, CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0) ([HF: hynt/F5-TTS-Vietnamese-ViVoice](https://huggingface.co/hynt/F5-TTS-Vietnamese-ViVoice)), and **viXTTS** (XTTS-v2 fine-tune, Coqui Public Model License, "subpar performance for Vietnamese sentences under 10 words" and incompatible with the upstream TTS library) ([HF: capleaf/viXTTS](https://huggingface.co/capleaf/viXTTS)). **VietTTS** (CosyVoice-derived, OpenAI-compatible streaming API, Vinorm normalization) has Apache-2.0 code but CC BY-NC models "based on an in-the-wild dataset" ([GitHub dangvansam/viet-tts](https://github.com/dangvansam/viet-tts)); **Valtec-TTS** (74.8M, five Northern/Southern voices, CPU RTF 0.24–0.47) is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([GitHub tronghieuit/valtec-tts](https://github.com/tronghieuit/valtec-tts)); **index-tts-2-vietnamese** claims Apache-2.0 yet also states "Commercial use requires permission from IndexTTS authors" ([HF: dinhthuan/index-tts-2-vietnamese](https://huggingface.co/dinhthuan/index-tts-2-vietnamese)); and Meta's **MMS-TTS vie** is a 36M CC-BY-NC VITS voice ([HF: facebook/mms-tts-vie](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-vie)).

Two permissive fallbacks exist below VieNeu. **zalopay/vietnamese-tts** is an F5-TTS fine-tune under **CC-BY-4.0**, the only F5-family Vietnamese checkpoint with a commercial license, but Zalopay itself reports it "may struggle with uncommon words or names," has "limited support for different accents or dialects" and produces duplicated-voice artifacts ([HF: zalopay/vietnamese-tts](https://huggingface.co/zalopay/vietnamese-tts)). The **Piper vi_VN-vais1000-medium** voice is a small ONNX VITS trained on the CC-BY 4.0 VAIS-1000 corpus in an MIT repo, "finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice" at 22.05 kHz ([Piper vais1000 MODEL_CARD](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/vi/vi_VN/vais1000/medium/MODEL_CARD)); it is a CPU-only, single-speaker, 2023-quality option.

There is no quality benchmark for any of this. No TTS arena includes Vietnamese samples, the only published MOS is index-tts-2-vietnamese's self-reported ~4.3 on an internal set, and the only academic comparison (ACL 2025) fine-tuned VALL-E, VoiceCraft and XTTS-v2 on PhoAudiobook and found the AR models "show superior performance in synthesizing short sentences" ([ACL Anthology 2025.acl-short.81](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-short.81/)). Text normalization is external for every model: VietTTS uses Vinorm, VieNeu uses sea-g2p, hynt trained on "normalized, lowercase text," and the March 2026 **VietNormalizer** (MIT, `pip install vietnormalizer`) exists precisely because most models do not handle numbers, dates, VND/USD currency, acronyms or loanword transliteration natively ([arXiv 2603.04145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04145)). Whichever model you choose, run VietNormalizer in front of it and enforce fully diacritized input.

## Packaging for MLflow and MinIO: safetensors beat .nemo, and licenses belong in the artifact

The file-format question largely decides the dependency tree. Every non-NVIDIA STT leader (Granite, Cohere, Qwen3-ASR, MOSS, ARK, Voxtral, Whisper, Kyutai-trfs) ships as safetensors loadable by Transformers, so a single Python service with `transformers` + `torch` can load all of them, and vLLM lists Whisper, Voxtral (with streaming via `/v1/realtime`), Qwen3-ASR, Granite Speech, MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize and Cohere-Transcribe as supported transcription models ([vLLM supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models.html)). NVIDIA's `.nemo` checkpoints are single tar archives that store equally well in MinIO, but they require the NeMo toolkit (minimum Python 3.12 and PyTorch 2.7 in the split-out NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech repo, with some checkpoints needing `weights_only=False` on PyTorch ≥2.6) and each model pins a different NeMo version, from 2.2 for Parakeet v2 to 26.06 for Nemotron-3.5 ([GitHub NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech)). Adding NeMo is worth it mainly for Parakeet's ~3,300 RTFx, Nemotron's cache-aware streaming, or parakeet-ctc-0.6b-Vietnamese. Whisper-family checkpoints (including PhoWhisper and BuzzASR) can be converted once to CTranslate2 for faster-whisper, which is "up to 4 times faster than openai/whisper for the same accuracy while using less memory" with int8 options ([GitHub SYSTRAN/faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)).

On the TTS side, pip-packaged models (`kokoro`, `chatterbox-tts`, VieNeu's package, `hume-tada`, `dia2`) and Transformers-native ones (Qwen3-TTS, MOSS-TTS) embed most easily; models whose reference stack is SGLang-Omni or vLLM-Omni (Fish, Higgs, Voxtral TTS, Breeze's fast path) are heavier to wrap in a plain pyfunc. "There is still no Ollama for TTS": vLLM-Omni, LocalAI and llama.cpp-omni each support different subsets ([Pinggy blog](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/)). F5-TTS-family Vietnamese checkpoints are single `.pt` files that depend on the F5-TTS repo and a runtime Vocos vocoder download, so the vocoder must be vendored into MinIO too for reproducible serving. viXTTS requires a forked TTS library and is a packaging liability.

Three operational rules follow from the gotchas. First, put a VAD front-end (Silero, integrated in both faster-whisper and whisper.cpp) and app-side chunking in the service rather than trusting each model's long-form mode: Whisper hallucinates on silence, ARK-ASR caps input at 30 s, Canary-Qwen and Phi-4 at 40 s, and Parakeet's local-attention long-form path has documented failures, including hallucinated output ~20% of the time with `modified_beam_search` in sherpa-onnx where `greedy_search` was correct ([sherpa-onnx issue #3267](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3267)). Second, if word timestamps are required, pick Parakeet TDT, Whisper/faster-whisper or Granite 4.1 2B-plus, or pair an LLM-decoder model with Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B, because Cohere Transcribe, ARK-ASR and MOSS-Transcribe emit none. Third, store the weight-repo license string and commit hash in the MLflow model metadata, since Whisper (Apache vs MIT), Step Audio EditX, Fun-CosyVoice3, index-tts-2-vietnamese and Omnilingual ASR all carry code/weight or card/paper license inconsistencies, and several TTS upstreams (Kokoro, Step Audio EditX, Zonos, Qwen3-TTS) are frozen or dormant, which argues for vendoring inference code alongside the weights.

For evaluation inside the registry, compute both orthographic and normalized WER on Vietnamese (diacritics preserved, digits spelled out, punctuation stripped, following the VietAudio leaderboard's rules) so punctuating and non-punctuating models compare fairly ([VietAudio-team dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/VietAudio-team/Vietnamese-asr-leaderboard)), and benchmark English on AMI and Earnings22, where every model's WER is 5–10x its LibriSpeech-clean figure and which most resemble real meeting and call audio.

## Conclusion

The four quadrants are not equally mature, and the registry design should reflect that asymmetry. STT in both languages is a solved procurement problem: permissive 2B models are now more accurate than Whisper large-v3 and the main engineering decision is whether to accept NeMo as a second dependency tree for Parakeet's throughput and Vietnamese timestamps. The pragmatic default is to register Qwen3-ASR-1.7B once as a bilingual model, add Granite Speech 4.1 2B if English accuracy on meeting audio justifies a second artifact, and revisit Parakeet-vi only if streaming or word timestamps become hard requirements for Vietnamese.

TTS is where license text, not Elo, determines the shortlist, and Vietnamese TTS exposes how thin the commercially clean supply is: a single 0.1B model from one developer who also sells a closed successor. Two implications follow. Build the serving layer so the model is swappable behind a fixed normalizer-plus-chunker pipeline (VietNormalizer for Vietnamese, sentence splitting under ~500 characters everywhere), because the Vietnamese pick is likely to change within a year as either MOSS-TTS-class multilingual models add Vietnamese at a usable size or a Vietnamese lab releases a clean-data model. And run your own blind listening test between VieNeu v3 Turbo and Viterbox before assuming the permissive option is the weaker one; the absence of any published Vietnamese TTS benchmark means that assumption is currently untested in either direction.

## Addendum (26 Sep 2026): models surfaced by the robo-be project

A sibling production project (Vietnamese children code-switching with English) evaluated several models in April 2026 that the sections above did not cover. Their current state was re-verified against Hugging Face, GitHub and arXiv on 26 Sep 2026; full notes are in `research_notes/.../robo_be_models_verification.md`.

Two findings correct the report above:

1. **A big multilingual open TTS does support Vietnamese.** `openbmb/VoxCPM2` (2.29B, Apache-2.0 for both code and weights, release 2.0.3 of May 2026) lists Vietnamese among 30 languages and publishes a Vietnamese WER of 3.31 with speaker similarity 80.6 on MiniMax-MLS, the best in its table. It streams, clones from a reference clip, and needs about 8 GB VRAM. It should sit next to VieNeu-TTS-v3-Turbo as a commercially safe Vietnamese TTS pick, and it covers English in the same model. Known open issues: a chirp artifact (#272) and a tokenizer change in the HF weights (#299).
2. **Cohere Transcribe 03-2026 is Apache-2.0 and covers Vietnamese.** `CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026` (2.07B, ~4.1 GB) is gated behind accepting terms and sharing contact details, but the card states Apache-2.0. It lists 14 languages including Vietnamese and English, scores 5.42 mean WER on the English Open ASR Leaderboard, and runs natively on transformers 5.4+ and vLLM. Vietnamese WER is published only as a chart, not a number.
